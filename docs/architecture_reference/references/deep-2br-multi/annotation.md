## Why this plan works

- The ground floor public zone uses the full plot width, so even on a narrow lot the living/dining/kitchen volume isn't squeezed by a corridor running alongside it; the corridor only appears where bedrooms need it, upstairs. [G]
- Two bedrooms upstairs is a small enough program that the landing stays short and direct, avoiding the long spine a bigger narrow-deep entry would need. [D]
- At 110 sqm over two floors, the plan matches the small program without adding a wing or hub the narrow lot couldn't fit anyway. [O]

## Trade-offs

- Using the full ground-floor width for the public zone leaves no ground-floor room for a guest bathroom separate from whatever's upstairs, a real program trade-off at this small a footprint. [F]

## Anti-patterns avoided

- A short, two-door upstairs landing rather than a stretched hall keeps this entry from needing a compact hub it has no room for, sidestepping [Hall spine without a compact hub](../../anti_patterns.md#2-hall-spine-without-a-compact-hub) by staying short in the first place.
