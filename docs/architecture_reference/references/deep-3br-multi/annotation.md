## Why this plan works

- Splitting the program across two storeys keeps each floor's footprint depth manageable on a narrow semi-detached lot, instead of pushing one single-storey plan to a depth the lot's proportions can't support well. [O]
- The public zone stays on the ground floor at a depth the narrow lot can actually hold; bedrooms move upstairs where the same footprint doesn't have to also carry the public program. [J]
- A stair that lands sensibly on both the ground-floor plan and the upper bedroom layout is the coupling this entry exists to represent on a narrow lot specifically. [M]

## Trade-offs

- Going to two storeys to manage depth on a narrow lot means the vertical core's position has less freedom than on a wider site; the stair has to work within a footprint that's already width-constrained on both levels. [M]

## Anti-patterns avoided

- Because both floors work within the same narrow width rather than one floor extending into a shape the other can't match, this entry avoids [Cross-level stair misalignment](../../anti_patterns.md#12-cross-level-stair-misalignment).
