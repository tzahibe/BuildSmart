## Why this plan works

- On a narrow, deep lot (depth/width ratio ~0.75), a single spine corridor is the organisation the site itself dictates; there isn't lot width for a hub-lobby or a second wing to branch from. [E]
- The corridor runs straight from the public zone to the bedrooms with no doubling back, keeping it as short as the lot depth allows even though it's a spine rather than a hub. [D]
- Every bedroom and the two bathrooms open directly onto the spine, so despite its length it still functions as genuine circulation, not as a room people must pass through to reach another room. [I]

## Trade-offs

- This is the deliberate case where a spine is the professionally correct answer, not a compromise, but it still carries the spine's structural cost: a longer hall long/short aspect and more circulation share than a hub-lobby plan of the same program would need, which is exactly why the census flags long single-loaded corridors as the dominant real-corpus gap on lots this narrow. [D]

## Anti-patterns avoided

- Because every room opens directly onto the spine rather than through a neighbouring bedroom, the plan avoids [Bedroom-to-bedroom access](../../anti_patterns.md#6-bedroom-to-bedroom-access) and [Chained circulation](../../anti_patterns.md#11-chained-circulation) even though its corridor is long.
