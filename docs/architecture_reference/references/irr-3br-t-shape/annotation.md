## Why this plan works

- The T's stem holds the shared circulation connecting both arms, giving the plan one clear, legible spine rather than circulation embedded inside either arm. [E]
- One arm is entirely public, the other entirely private; the T's own geometry enforces a zoning split that would otherwise have to be imposed on a rectangle by convention alone. [J]
- Because the stem is the only shared space between the two arms, the transition between public and private zones happens at exactly one point. [J]

## Trade-offs

- A T-shape's stem is itself a corridor-like space by construction; it's harder for this footprint to avoid a spine-like circulation element than for a hub-lobby rectangle, even though the stem is doing real zoning work the rectangle's hub wouldn't. [D]

## Anti-patterns avoided

- The public arm and private arm meeting only at the stem, with no room in either arm required to pass through the other arm, avoids [Chained circulation](../../anti_patterns.md#11-chained-circulation) between the two zones.
