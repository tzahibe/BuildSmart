## Why this plan works

- The ground floor wraps the courtyard on three sides, giving public and service rooms courtyard exposure in addition to the outer envelope. [C]
- The upper floor sits over one wing only, rather than over the whole courtyard footprint; the second storey is added exactly where the bedroom program needs it, not as a uniform cap over three wings that don't need it. [L]
- Because only one wing carries a second storey, the vertical core only has to seat sensibly within that wing's ground-to-first-floor relationship, not across the full three-wing ring. [M]

## Trade-offs

- Confining the upper floor to one wing means that wing carries the full structural load of the second storey alone, while the other two ground-floor wings get none of the load-sharing a uniform two-storey courtyard building would have, a real structural trade-off for keeping the massing need-proportional. [M]

## Anti-patterns avoided

- Seating the vertical core within the single two-storey wing, rather than spanning it across the whole courtyard ring, keeps this entry's stair-seating problem local and avoids [Cross-level stair misalignment](../../anti_patterns.md#12-cross-level-stair-misalignment).
