## Why this plan works

- The footprint's angle follows the plot's own irregular boundary rather than inscribing a rectangle inside it and wasting the site's non-rectangular area. [O]
- Following the plot boundary this closely is what the 21-plan census's non-rectangular majority (13 of 21) actually looks like in practice; this entry represents that pattern at the larger end of the program range. [O]
- Five bedrooms and three bathrooms still fit the angled envelope without any individual room being forced into an unusually acute or obtuse shape to fill the corner the angle creates. [A]

## Trade-offs

- An angled/cross footprint has more distinct wall angles to detail and structure than a rectangle or even an L, and each angled corner is a place a room's proportion can be squeezed into something less regular than the rubric's target band, a real design-discipline cost the plot's irregularity imposes. [A]

## Anti-patterns avoided

- Keeping every room's proportions inside the plan's angled corners rather than letting the corners produce elongated leftover rooms avoids [Strip-shaped public room](../../anti_patterns.md#1-strip-shaped-public-room) forming at the angle.
