## Why this plan works

- The L's two wings put bedrooms in their own arm, entirely separate from the wing that holds the public zone, a zoning boundary that's structural, not just a hallway distinction. [J]
- The corner plot is what makes the L massing pay off: the L's inner corner forms a genuine entry court instead of leftover exterior area, using the corner condition the site actually offers. [L]
- Circulation crosses the seam between the two wings through a single hall junction, giving the plan one clear point where public and private paths meet rather than several. [E]

## Trade-offs

- An L massing costs more perimeter wall per square metre of floor area than an equivalent rectangle, and the seam between wings is a structural and circulation pinch point that a single rectangle wouldn't have; costs an L should only be worth paying when, as here, the corner plot actually rewards it. [L]

## Anti-patterns avoided

- The corner-plot entry court is a real, used exterior space rather than a residual notch beside the door, avoiding [Entrance dead-end](../../anti_patterns.md#7-entrance-dead-end).
