## Why this plan works

- Only the wing that needs a second storey (to hold the bedrooms) gets one; the single-storey wing stays single-storey rather than the whole footprint being raised to match. [L]
- Bedrooms occupy the taller wing's upper floor as one coherent group, so the vertical core only has to seat sensibly within that one wing rather than across the full L. [M]
- Confining the two-storey portion to one wing means the ground-floor L massing (both wings) stays legible from outside, rather than a uniform box hiding which parts are single- vs double-height. [L]

## Trade-offs

- Mixing storey heights across wings means the roofline and structural transition between wings is more complex to build than either a uniform single-storey L or a uniform two-storey L, a real construction cost for confining the second storey to just the bedroom wing. [M]

## Anti-patterns avoided

- Because the vertical core only needs to seat within the single taller wing rather than across the whole L footprint, this entry has an easier time avoiding [Cross-level stair misalignment](../../anti_patterns.md#12-cross-level-stair-misalignment) than a plan that raised both wings.
