## Why this plan works

- The safe room sits in the bedroom wing, sharing that wing's direct corridor access like every other bedroom, rather than being placed in the entry-court wing where it would interrupt the public/arrival sequence. [J]
- Keeping the safe room away from the entry-court wing means the L's inner corner and entry sequence stay dedicated to arrival, not shared with a structurally-constrained bedroom. [H]
- The two-wing zoning (bedrooms vs. entry court) gives the safe room's placement a clear rule to follow; which wing it belongs in is unambiguous given the L's own zone split. [J]

## Trade-offs

- Confining the safe room to the bedroom wing means that wing's structural reinforcement requirement can't be traded off against the entry-court wing's massing; the L's two-wing split, useful for zoning, also means the safe-room constraint is entirely local to one wing with no flexibility to shift it to the other. [A]

## Anti-patterns avoided

- Placing the safe room in the bedroom wing with direct corridor access, not behind the entry-court wing's arrival sequence, avoids both [Room-in-a-room](../../anti_patterns.md#4-room-in-a-room) access and an [Entrance dead-end](../../anti_patterns.md#7-entrance-dead-end)-style compromise of the arrival wing.
