## Why this plan works

- The L only appears because the plot can hold a rectangle at roughly 0.6x the total area plus a second wing sharing a full side; the massing is sized to what the site can actually support, not chosen independent of the plot. [O]
- The second wing shares a full side with the main rectangle rather than meeting it at a point, giving the seam real structural and circulation width instead of a pinched corner. [L]
- The L's inner corner is used for a sheltered terrace, genuine outdoor program, not leftover area the shape happened to produce. [L]

## Trade-offs

- Needing a plot that can hold both the main rectangle and a second full-side wing is a real site precondition; this massing doesn't transfer to a plot too narrow or too small for that 0.6x-plus-wing geometry, unlike the single-rectangle entries in this set. [O]

## Anti-patterns avoided

- The L earns its second wing from a specific site-fit calculation rather than being offered "because it's more interesting," which is exactly the discipline [Unconditional L-massing slot](../../anti_patterns.md#10-unconditional-l-massing-slot) names the absence of.
