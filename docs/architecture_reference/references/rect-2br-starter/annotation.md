## Why this plan works

- At 90 sqm, the plan keeps to a single rectangle rather than adding wings or a hub it has no area budget for, matching massing to program size instead of manufacturing complexity for a small house. [L]
- The public zone (living/dining/kitchen) is planned as one open, contiguous volume rather than three separate rooms that happen to be adjacent. [G]
- With only one shared bathroom, the plan keeps it on the shortest possible path from both bedrooms rather than isolating it at the far end of the plan. [F]
- The compact footprint keeps circulation area small in absolute terms even without a formal hub, since only two bedrooms need to be reached from the entry. [D]

## Trade-offs

- A single shared bathroom means no bedroom gets private ensuite access, an explicit area/program trade-off appropriate at this size, but a real reduction in privacy compared with the larger entries in this set. [F]

## Anti-patterns avoided

- The open-plan public zone is planned as one group from the start, avoiding [Strip-shaped public room](../../anti_patterns.md#1-strip-shaped-public-room) fragmentation into disconnected living/dining/kitchen boxes.
