## Why this plan works

- The near-square footprint ratio (~0.95-1.05) keeps the overall massing close to the shape the room-proportion rubric target favors, avoiding the elongated envelope that forces rooms into strip proportions to hit their area. [A]
- A single, direct corridor connects every room without doubling back or serving as anything other than circulation, keeping the plan legible even without a compact hub. [D]
- The master bedroom's ensuite bathroom sits directly off its own room, and the two remaining bedrooms share a family bathroom on the same corridor run, so every wet room is within one or two doors of another. [F]
- Bedrooms, the shared bathroom and the ensuite form one contiguous private wing off the corridor, cleanly separated from the public zone at the entry end. [J]

## Trade-offs

- A straight spine, not a compact hub-lobby, is the circulation choice here. It is simpler to build and site but does not deliver the ~2.5-3.5 m compact-hub topology the census shows most professional plans converge on ([D], see anti-pattern #2 below).

## Anti-patterns avoided

- Because every bedroom and the shared bathroom open directly onto the corridor rather than onto each other, no room's only route passes through another private room, avoiding [Chained circulation](../../anti_patterns.md#11-chained-circulation) and [Bedroom-to-bedroom access](../../anti_patterns.md#6-bedroom-to-bedroom-access).
