## Why this plan works

- The ground floor is planned as one open-plan public zone rather than split into separate boxed rooms, giving the household's main living space one continuous volume. [G]
- All three bedrooms sit upstairs around a shared landing, keeping the private program on one level and the public program on the other rather than interleaving them. [J]
- The landing functions as the upstairs hub, a genuine junction bedrooms branch from, not a corridor stretched between them. [D]
- A stair position that works from both the ground-floor public zone and the upstairs landing is the deliberate constraint this entry represents: the vertical core has to seat sensibly on both floors, not just one. [M]

## Trade-offs

- Splitting public and private program across two levels means every trip between them goes through the stair; there is exactly one vertical connector, and its position has far less design freedom than a single-level hub's does. [M]

## Anti-patterns avoided

- A shared landing that every bedroom branches from directly, rather than a stair that dead-ends into the first bedroom's door, avoids [Cross-level stair misalignment](../../anti_patterns.md#12-cross-level-stair-misalignment)-style awkward upstairs arrival.
