## Why this plan works

- The hub-lobby organisation keeps the private wing's depth constant as the bedroom count grows from three to six; bedrooms are added by extending the wing along the hub, not by deepening the footprint into a strip. [D]
- Four bathrooms are distributed across the wing so that no bedroom is more than one hub-branch away from a wet room, rather than concentrating all plumbing at one end. [F]
- At 260 sqm, the plan stays a single rectangle rather than fragmenting into wings it doesn't need; massing complexity tracks what the program actually requires. [L]

## Trade-offs

- Six bedrooms sharing one hub means the hub itself has to carry more door traffic than a three- or four-bedroom version; past a certain bedroom count, a single hub starts trading its compactness for the number of doors converging on it. [D]

## Anti-patterns avoided

- Keeping wing depth constant as bedrooms are added, rather than stretching the corridor to reach each new room, avoids the plan degrading into [Hall spine without a compact hub](../../anti_patterns.md#2-hall-spine-without-a-compact-hub) at the larger program size.
