## Why this plan works

- Six bedrooms split across two side-by-side wings keeps each wing's corridor short, three bedrooms per wing, instead of one corridor long enough to reach all six in sequence. [D]
- Four bathrooms split between the two wings means each wing gets its own wet-room access rather than sharing one distant bathroom across the whole plan. [F]
- The broad plot this footprint family assumes is what makes two parallel wings possible at all; the massing choice is a direct response to the site, not an arbitrary complexity increase. [O]

## Trade-offs

- Two wings mean two separate circulation runs to maintain rather than one, more total corridor area than a single-spine six-bedroom plan, traded for keeping each wing's reach short. [D]

## Anti-patterns avoided

- Splitting into two three-bedroom wings instead of one six-bedroom spine avoids the far end of the plan turning into [Hall spine without a compact hub](../../anti_patterns.md#2-hall-spine-without-a-compact-hub) at this program size.
